# inculva — Accessibility Laws & Regulations

## Overview

Web accessibility is not just good practice — it's increasingly required by law. Many countries and regions have enacted legislation requiring websites and digital services to be accessible to people with disabilities. inculva helps organizations comply with these regulations.

## Key Regulations

### ADA — Americans with Disabilities Act (United States)

- **Scope:** Title III applies to "places of public accommodation," which courts have increasingly interpreted to include websites.
- **Standard:** References WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the technical standard.
- **Enforcement:** Lawsuits can be filed by individuals. ADA web accessibility lawsuits have increased significantly year over year.
- **Penalties:** Monetary damages, attorney fees, and injunctive relief (court order to fix the website).

### Section 508 (United States)

- **Scope:** Applies to federal government websites and any technology purchased by federal agencies.
- **Standard:** Directly references WCAG 2.0 Level AA (updated via ICT Refresh in 2018).
- **Enforcement:** Complaints can be filed with the relevant federal agency or through lawsuits.

### EAA — European Accessibility Act (European Union)

- **Scope:** Covers products and services including websites, mobile apps, e-commerce, banking, transportation, and e-books.
- **Effective:** June 28, 2025 — organizations must be compliant by this date.
- **Standard:** References EN 301 549, which maps to WCAG 2.1 Level AA.
- **Enforcement:** Each EU member state establishes its own enforcement mechanism.

### EN 301 549 (European Union)

- **Scope:** European standard for ICT (Information and Communications Technology) accessibility.
- **Standard:** Incorporates WCAG 2.1 Level AA plus additional requirements for non-web ICT (software, hardware, documentation).
- **Usage:** Referenced by the EAA, public procurement directives, and national laws across EU member states.

### AODA — Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (Canada)

- **Scope:** Applies to organizations in Ontario, Canada.
- **Standard:** References WCAG 2.0 Level AA.
- **Enforcement:** Fines up to $100,000/day for organizations that fail to comply.

### Equality Act 2010 (United Kingdom)

- **Scope:** Requires service providers to make "reasonable adjustments" for disabled people, including digital services.
- **Standard:** References WCAG 2.1 Level AA as best practice.
- **Enforcement:** Legal action can be taken through courts or the Equality and Human Rights Commission.

## Why Compliance Matters

### Legal Risk

Web accessibility lawsuits are increasing globally. In the United States alone, thousands of ADA-related web accessibility lawsuits are filed each year. Non-compliance can result in financial penalties, legal fees, and mandatory remediation.

### Business Impact

- **Market reach:** Over 1 billion people worldwide have disabilities. Inaccessible websites exclude these potential customers.
- **SEO benefits:** Many accessibility practices (alt text, semantic HTML, heading structure) also improve search engine optimization.
- **Brand reputation:** Demonstrating commitment to inclusion builds trust and positive brand perception.
- **Usability:** Accessibility improvements benefit all users, not just those with disabilities (e.g., mobile users, elderly users, users in challenging environments).
